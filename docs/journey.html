Belief Journey Map

The project asks how ordinary people conceptualise democracy, what they expect from it, and how their attitudes translate into behaviour. This interview follows those three questions as three modules and codes every answer with one codebook. That makes the gaps between what democracy is to someone, what they expect, and what they would do measurable. Follow one conversation from each sentence to the finished map, then see the same journey for a whole population.

Example transcripts are hand-written personas (two set in Bangladesh); the population is synthetic. Not real findings.

Everyone's journey

The same three modules for 300 synthetic respondents: how they first describe democracy, what they put first, and what they do in the two scenarios. Bands are coloured by the first description. Hover a band to see how many people take that path.

How it is coded. Every answer is coded into 11 considerations with the repo's transparent keyword annotator (the same code as analysis/annotate.py, ported to this page). Concept: named, named with a reason, or essential (a free press judged indispensable). Expectation: named, or chosen as the priority in the elections-vs-development trade-off. Reality: judged clause by clause as delivered well or badly. Behaviour: the vote between a builder who jails journalists and an honest candidate, and what the person would do if their own party postponed an election. An action paradox is a principle named in module A and acted against in module C.

The bridge question. When an action paradox appears, the Bridge Agent asks one neutral question putting the two statements side by side, then codes whether the person reconciles, rationalises or revises. Because it could shift answers, it runs only in a randomly assigned half of interviews. Built by Tansif Anzar as a mockup for the Emmy Noether project "Predictably Paradoxical".
