Context Lens

People in Bangladesh, India and Germany talk about democracy in different words, worry about different risks when they answer, and can mean different things by the same answer. A context pack lets one instrument travel without importing one society's assumptions into another. The construct each question measures stays fixed; the wording, the vocabulary the coder recognises, the agents' conduct and the guardrails for interpretation adapt. Every country gets a pack, Germany included, so no society is the default.

Context:
Packs are drafts to be reviewed by local experts and pre-tested; example answers are synthetic.
1 · Wording

Same construct, local wording

The behaviour scenarios are rewritten so they sound like real local politics. Green marks local wording, struck-through text is the generic version it replaces. What the question measures does not change.

2 · Vocabulary

The coder learns local political vocabulary

Local terms are mapped to the shared codebook, so a hartal in Dhaka and a bandh in Mumbai are both coded as order, and a caretaker government as elections. Pick an example or type your own answer.

3 · Conduct

When a question feels risky to answer

On sensitive topics, a refusal can signal perceived risk rather than a lack of opinion. Without a pack, a probe agent sees a short answer and pushes. With the pack, it reassures and moves on, and the refusal is recorded as its own outcome.

4 · Interpretation

Guardrails for analysis and write-up

The same answer can mean different things in different places. These notes travel with the data, so that a reading from one society is not applied to another.
